# Mars Background — Physical Properties, Geology & Atmosphere

## Physical Properties

### Size & Mass
| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Equatorial radius | 3,396.2 km (53% of Earth) |
| Polar radius | 3,376.2 km |
| Mass | 6.417 × 10²³ kg (10.7% of Earth) |
| Surface area | ~144.8 million km² (≈ Earth's dry land area) |
| Mean density | 3.93 g/cm³ (vs. Earth 5.51) |
| Surface gravity | 3.72 m/s² (0.38 g) |
| Escape velocity | 5.03 km/s |

### Internal Structure
- **Core**: Radius ~1,830 km (liquid outer core confirmed by InSight seismology, 2021); iron-nickel-sulfur with dissolved lighter elements (H, O, C)
- **Mantle**: Olivine-rich silicate, 770–1,170 km thick
- **Crust**: Average ~45 km; thinner in north (~32 km), thicker in south (~58 km)
- **No global magnetic field** today; remnant crustal magnetism in southern highlands indicates a dynamo active ~4 Ga

### Seasons
| Season (N. Hemisphere) | Duration | Ls Range |
|------------------------|----------|----------|
| Northern Spring | 194 sols (longest) | 0°–90° |
| Northern Summer | 178 sols | 90°–180° |
| Northern Autumn | 142 sols (shortest) | 180°–270° |
| Northern Winter | 154 sols | 270°–360° |

Mars eccentricity makes southern summers hotter and shorter (at perihelion).

### Temperature
- **Range**: −153°C (poles, winter) to +20°C (equatorial noon)
- **Mean surface**: −60°C
- **Diurnal swing**: Up to 100°C at equatorial latitudes

### Moons
- **Phobos**: 26.8 × 22.4 × 18.4 km; orbits 9,376 km; spiraling inward; will disintegrate in ~50 Myr
- **Deimos**: 15 × 12.2 × 10.4 km; orbits 23,463 km; likely captured C-type asteroids

## Geology

### Geologic Time Scale
| Eon | Age | Key Events |
|-----|-----|------------|
| Noachian | 4.1–3.7 Ga | Heavy bombardment, valley networks, phyllosilicate formation, possible ocean |
| Hesperian | 3.7–3.0 Ga | Volcanic plains, catastrophic floods, sulfate formation, atmospheric loss |
| Amazonian | 3.0 Ga–present | Cold/dry, glacial activity, limited volcanism, dust storms |

### Crustal Dichotomy
The most fundamental geological feature: hemispheric divide between southern highlands (cratered, ancient, 1–3 km higher) and northern lowlands (smooth volcanic plains, younger). Andrews-Hanna et al. (2008, Nature) showed the boundary fits a ~10,600 × 8,500 km ellipse — consistent with the Borealis giant impact.

### Tharsis Volcanic Province
- ~5,000 km across, rises ~10 km above mean datum
- Hosts Olympus Mons (21.9 km high, 600 km base — largest volcano in solar system)
- Three Tharsis Montes: Ascraeus (18.2 km), Pavonis (14 km), Arsia (17.8 km)
- Alba Mons: largest areal footprint (~2,000 km)
- Tectonic effects: Radial graben including Valles Marineris

### Valles Marineris
- Length: ~4,000 km; Width: up to 600 km; Depth: up to 10 km
- Origin: tectonic rifting from Tharsis uplift (not water-carved, though later modified by water)
- Interior layered deposits contain sulfate minerals, evidence of ancient lakes

### Major Impact Basins
| Basin | Diameter | Notable Features |
|-------|----------|-----------------|
| Hellas Planitia | ~2,300 km | Deepest on Mars (−8.2 km); highest surface pressure |
| Argyre Planitia | ~1,800 km | Glacial features on margins |
| Isidis Planitia | ~1,500 km | Hosts Jezero Crater on western rim |
| Borealis Basin | ~10,600 km | Proposed; may form entire northern lowlands |

## Atmosphere

### Composition
| Gas | Abundance |
|-----|-----------|
| CO₂ | 95.32% |
| N₂ | 2.6% |
| Ar | 1.9% |
| O₂ | 0.16% |
| CO | 0.06% |
| H₂O | 0.03% (variable) |
| CH₄ | Trace (~0.5 ppb seasonal, disputed) |

### Properties
- Surface pressure: 600–700 Pa (0.6% of Earth); varies ±25% seasonally
- Scale height: ~11 km
- Highest pressure: Hellas Basin floor (~1,155 Pa)
- Sky color: Butterscotch/tan from suspended iron oxide dust

### CO₂ Cycle
~25–30% of the atmosphere freezes onto winter polar cap each year, causing global pressure variations of ~170 Pa.

### Dust Storms
- **Local**: Common, especially during southern summer
- **Global dust storms (GDS)**: Every 3–4 Mars years; last months; reduce solar insolation >99%
- 2018 GDS ended Opportunity mission; 1971 GDS greeted Mariner 9
- Dust devils: up to 8 km tall

### Atmospheric Loss (MAVEN)
- Current loss: ~1–2 kg/s escaping to space
- Mechanisms: Solar wind sputtering, photochemical escape, ion pickup
- Total lost: ≥0.8 bar CO₂ over Mars history (Jakosky et al. 2018)
- CMEs increase escape 10–100×
