# Additional Mars Instruments — TES, OMEGA, GRS, MARSIS & Others

## TES — Thermal Emission Spectrometer

- **Mission**: Mars Global Surveyor (MGS); operated 1997–2006
- **PI**: Philip Christensen (ASU)
- **Type**: Fourier transform infrared spectrometer + broadband thermal bolometers
- **Spectral range**: 6–50 μm (200–1,700 cm⁻¹); 10 cm⁻¹ spectral sampling
- **Spatial resolution**: ~3 km/pixel (nadir); 8 km/pixel (limb)
- **Key discoveries**:
  - **Hematite in Meridiani Planum** (Christensen et al. 2000, Science): coarse-grained gray hematite signature at 300 cm⁻¹; led to Opportunity landing site selection
  - Global surface mineral maps: basalt (high-Ca pyroxene) dominates lowlands; andesite or weathered basalt in highlands
  - Global thermal inertia and albedo maps at ~3 km/pixel — foundational dataset
  - Atmospheric dust and water ice opacity profiles; seasonal CO₂ frost maps
- **Limitations**: 3 km/pixel insufficient to resolve individual outcrops; superseded by CRISM for mineralogy

## OMEGA — Observatoire pour la Minéralogie, l'Eau, les Glaces et l'Activité

- **Mission**: Mars Express (ESA); launched June 2003, operational 2004–present
- **PI**: Jean-Pierre Bibring (IAS, Orsay)
- **Type**: Imaging spectrometer (VNIR + SWIR + IR)
- **Spectral range**: 0.35–5.1 μm; 352 spectral channels
- **Spatial resolution**: 0.3–5 km/pixel (altitude-dependent)
- **Key discoveries**:
  - **First orbital detection of hydrated minerals on Mars** (Bibring et al. 2005, Science): phyllosilicates in Noachian terrains
  - Global phyllosilicate distribution: Fe/Mg smectites, chlorites, prehnite in ancient highlands
  - Sulfate deposits in Hesperian terrains: polyhydrated and monohydrated sulfates in Valles Marineris, Terra Meridiani
  - Water ice and CO₂ ice seasonal mapping at polar caps
  - Proposed "phyllosian–theiikian–siderikian" mineralogical epochs of Mars history
- **Spectral bands of interest**: 1.9 μm (H₂O), 2.3 μm (Fe/Mg-OH), 2.5 μm (carbonate), 3.4 μm (organics/carbonate)

## GRS — Gamma Ray Spectrometer

- **Mission**: Mars Odyssey; launched 2001, operational 2002–present
- **PI**: William Boynton (University of Arizona)
- **Components**: Gamma Ray Spectrometer (GRS), Neutron Spectrometer (NS), High Energy Neutron Detector (HEND)
- **Spatial resolution**: ~600 km (footprint); measures bulk composition of top ~1 m of regolith
- **Key discoveries**:
  - **Subsurface hydrogen enrichment at high latitudes** (Boynton et al. 2002, Science): epithermal neutron suppression indicates water equivalent hydrogen (WEH) > 10% by mass poleward of ±60°
  - Confirmed water ice table within 1 m of surface at high latitudes — later confirmed by Phoenix lander (2008)
  - Global elemental maps: K, Th, Fe, Si, Cl, H — constrain crustal composition and volatile distribution
  - Chlorine enrichment in low-albedo regions; potassium/thorium ratio maps crustal evolution
- **Detection principle**: Cosmic rays produce neutrons in regolith; hydrogen moderates epithermal neutrons → suppressed flux detected from orbit

## MARSIS — Mars Advanced Radar for Subsurface and Ionosphere Sounding

- **Mission**: Mars Express (ESA); deployed May 2005
- **PI**: Giovanni Picardi (Sapienza University); later Roberto Orosei (INAF)
- **Type**: Synthetic aperture radar sounder
- **Frequency**: 1.8–5 MHz (4 sub-bands); ionosphere sounding at 0.1–5.5 MHz
- **Depth penetration**: 1–5 km in ice; ~1 km in dry basalt
- **Vertical resolution**: ~150 m in free space; ~50 m in ice
- **Key discoveries**:
  - South Polar Layered Deposits (SPLD) internal structure: ice-rich layers, basal unit
  - **Controversial "subglacial lake" at Ultimi Scopuli** (Orosei et al. 2018, Science): bright basal reflection at 1.5 km depth beneath SPLD interpreted as liquid water; disputed — alternative explanations include conductive minerals (smectite, iron oxides) or CO₂ ice
  - Multiple additional bright basal reflections reported (Lauro et al. 2021); debate ongoing
  - Ionospheric electron density profiles; solar wind interaction mapping
- **Antenna**: Two 20 m dipole booms deployed in orbit

## SHARAD — SHAllow RADar

- **Mission**: Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter (MRO); launched 2005
- **PI**: Roberto Seu (Sapienza University)
- **Frequency**: 15–25 MHz (center 20 MHz)
- **Depth penetration**: ~1 km in ice; ~100 m in dry regolith
- **Vertical resolution**: ~15 m in free space; ~8 m in ice
- **Key findings**: North Polar Layered Deposits (NPLD) stratigraphy; mid-latitude ice deposits (Plaut et al. 2009); buried impact craters in northern lowlands
- **Comparison with MARSIS**: SHARAD = shallower penetration, higher resolution; MARSIS = deeper penetration, lower resolution

## MCS — Mars Climate Sounder

- **Mission**: Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter (MRO); operational 2006–present
- **Type**: Limb-viewing infrared radiometer; 9 spectral channels (0.3–32 μm)
- **Measurements**: Atmospheric temperature profiles (10–80 km altitude), dust opacity, water ice opacity, CO₂ condensation
- **Vertical resolution**: ~5 km; horizontal resolution ~5 km along-track
- **Key products**: Global atmospheric temperature maps; dust storm vertical structure; polar vortex characterization

## CaSSIS — Colour and Stereo Surface Imaging System

- **Mission**: ExoMars Trace Gas Orbiter (TGO, ESA/Roscosmos); operational 2018–present
- **PI**: Nicolas Thomas (University of Bern)
- **Resolution**: 4.5 m/pixel (from 400 km altitude)
- **Stereo**: Single-pass stereo via rotation of spacecraft; ~1 m vertical precision DTMs
- **Spectral filters**: 4 bands (BLU 400–550 nm, PAN 675–775 nm, RED 836–927 nm, NIR 985–1088 nm)
- **Swath width**: 9.4 km
- **Capability**: Color stereo at HiRISE-comparable resolution; broader swath than HiRISE

## NOMAD + ACS — Trace Gas Spectrometers on TGO

- **Mission**: ExoMars TGO; operational 2018–present
- **NOMAD**: Nadir and Occultation for MArs Discovery; UV-visible-IR (0.2–3.8 μm); solar occultation + nadir
- **ACS**: Atmospheric Chemistry Suite; 0.7–17 μm; three spectrometer channels
- **Primary goal**: Detect and characterize trace gases including CH₄, H₂O, HDO, HCl, SO₂
- **Methane status**: No confirmed methane detection at TGO sensitivity limit of ~0.05 ppb (Korablev et al. 2019, Nature) — in tension with Curiosity in-situ detections (~0.5 ppb seasonal)
- **HCl detection**: First detection of HCl on Mars (Korablev et al. 2021, Science Advances); linked to dust storm chemistry
