# Mars Ice Science — Detection, Distribution & ISRU Relevance

## Polar Ice Caps

### North Polar Cap (Planum Boreum)
- Permanent water ice cap, ~1,000 km diameter, ~3 km thick
- Seasonal CO₂ frost (up to 1 m) in winter
- ~1.6 million km³ water ice (equivalent to ~35 m global ocean)
- Polar layered deposits (NPLD) record climate over millions of years
- Chasma Boreale: 560 km canyon cutting through cap exposes layer cross-sections

### South Polar Cap (Planum Australe)
- Permanent CO₂ ice cap (~8 m thick) overlying water ice
- ~400 km permanent residual cap
- Swiss-cheese terrain from CO₂ sublimation pits
- MARSIS detected ~20 km wide bright radar reflection at ~1.5 km depth (Orosei et al. 2018) — debated whether liquid water, conductive clays, or layered ice
- SHARAD (2025) found only faint signal at same location — discrepancy suggests non-liquid origin

## Subsurface Ice Distribution

### SWIM Project (USGS)
The Subsurface Water Ice Mapping project (Morgan et al. 2021, Nature Astronomy) integrated five datasets at 200 m/pixel:
- Thermal inertia
- Epithermal neutron flux
- Radar dielectric properties
- Radar geomorphology
- Surface geomorphology

Key results:
- Extensive buried ice across mid-latitudes (30°–60° N/S)
- Ice depths: 0–1 m, 1–5 m, and >5 m categories
- Arcadia Planitia: ice_consistency 0.78–0.92, depth 1–5 m
- Utopia Planitia: ice_consistency 0.92, depth ~1.8 m

### Key Ice Deposits
| Region | Evidence | Ice Depth | Volume |
|--------|----------|-----------|--------|
| Arcadia Planitia | SHARAD reflectors, fresh craters, polygons | 1–5 m | ~2.4×10⁴ km³ (Bramson 2017) |
| Utopia Planitia | SHARAD massive deposit, scalloped terrain | ~1.8 m | ~83,000 km³ (Stuurman 2016) |
| Deuteronilus Mensae | Lobate debris aprons (rock glaciers) | Under debris | Significant |
| Mid-latitude craters | Fresh impact exposures (Dundas 2014) | 1–10 m | Widespread |

### Ice Detection Methods
1. **SHARAD radar sounding**: Subsurface reflectors from dielectric contrast; ε' ≈ 3.15 for pure ice vs. 4–6 for dry regolith
2. **Neutron spectroscopy** (Mars Odyssey GRS): Hydrogen abundance; >40 wt% H₂O-equivalent at 40–50°N
3. **Fresh impact craters**: HiRISE imaging reveals clean ice exposed at 1–10 m depth
4. **Geomorphology**: Polygonal terrain (thermal contraction), scalloped depressions (sublimation)
5. **Thermal inertia**: TES/THEMIS measurements; ice-cemented regolith 150–300 J m⁻² K⁻¹ s⁻½
6. **CRISM spectral**: 1.5 µm and 2.0 µm H₂O absorptions; 1.25 µm Fresnel reflection peak

### Dielectric Constant Interpretation
| εr Range | Material | Notes |
|----------|----------|-------|
| 3.0–3.2 | Pure water ice | Clean, solid H₂O |
| 2.5–3.0 | Porous ice-rich regolith | High porosity + ice |
| 4–6 | Dry basaltic regolith | Typical Mars surface |
| 6–9 | Dense basalt | Intact volcanic rock |

Reference: Pure water ice εr ≈ 3.15 (Matsuoka et al. 1997)

## Key Research Findings

### Bramson et al. 2015, GRL
Detected massive subsurface ice at Arcadia Planitia. Excess ice extends to ~170 m depth. Dielectric constant ε'=3.0 (pure water ice). Best-fit models require >80% ice purity.

### Bramson et al. 2017, JGR
Mapped buried ice in Arcadia and Utopia. Thickest deposits (>100 m) at 43–48°N. Volume: 2.4×10⁴ km³ in Arcadia.

### Dundas et al. 2018, Science
8 mid-latitude ice scarps at 55–58°N. Ice purity >90%. Scarp retreat ~1–2 mm/yr from sublimation.

### Dundas et al. 2014, JGR
Fresh impact craters at 39–55°N expose clean water ice at 1–10 m depth. Craters at 43°N in Arcadia show ice within 1–2 m.

### Morgan et al. 2021, Nature Astronomy
SWIM v2: Integrated 5 datasets at 200 m/pixel. Arcadia scores: ice_consistency 0.78–0.92, depth_confidence high.

### Feldman et al. 2004, JGR
Mars Odyssey neutron spectrometer: >40% water-equivalent hydrogen by mass in upper 1 m at 40–50°N.

### Mellon & Jakosky 1995, JGR
Thermal model: ice table at 5–20 cm depth above 50°N, deepening to 1–5 m at 40°N.

## Ice Table Stability

### Current Stability
- Schorghofer (2007, Nature): Ice table oscillates with obliquity; current ice receding equatorward from ~30°N since last high-obliquity epoch (~5 Ma)
- Arcadia at 45°N is well within the current stability zone
- Putzig et al. (2005): TES thermal inertia in Arcadia 150–300 J m⁻² K⁻¹ s⁻½ (fine-grained ice-cemented regolith over pure ice)

### Recent Findings (2025–2026)
- Near-surface brines inferred from seasonal marsquakes (Nature Communications, Dec 2025): ice-to-brine phase transitions north of 30°N
- Penn State study (Feb 2026): amino acids survive 50 Myr in clean Martian ice; future missions should target clean buried ice
- Comprehensive water ice review published (Space Science Reviews, Dec 2025)

## ISRU Relevance

### Water Extraction
- Most accessible ice: Arcadia Planitia and Utopia Planitia (1–5 m depth)
- Drilling technology: 2 m drill (ExoMars Rosalind Franklin); deeper drilling needed for ISRU
- Recommended depth: 1–5 m to reach massive ice (Dundas et al. 2021)

### Propellant Production
- Sabatier reaction: CO₂ + 4H₂ → CH₄ + 2H₂O
- Electrolysis: H₂O → H₂ + ½O₂
- MOXIE (Perseverance): Demonstrated O₂ from CO₂; produced 122 g total

### Landing Site Ranking for ISRU
1. Arcadia Planitia — shallow ice, flat terrain, moderate latitude
2. Utopia Planitia — confirmed massive ice, flat, but high latitude
3. Deuteronilus Mensae — debris-covered glaciers, rough terrain
