# Mars Mineralogy — Key Mineral Groups, Spectral Signatures & Distribution

## Overview

Mars has a basaltic upper crust with regionally variable plagioclase, pyroxene, and olivine. Secondary minerals record the planet's aqueous alteration history and serve as key indicators of past habitability (Ehlmann & Edwards 2014, Annual Review of Earth and Planetary Sciences).

## Primary Igneous Minerals

### Pyroxenes
- **Low-Ca pyroxene (LCP)**: Enstatite/ferrosilite; dominant in ancient highlands
- **High-Ca pyroxene (HCP)**: Augite/pigeonite; dominant in younger volcanic plains
- Spectral signatures: 1.0 µm and 2.0 µm Fe²⁺ crystal field absorptions
- Distribution: Global; composition varies with age and volcanic province

### Olivine
- Spectral signature: Broad 1.0 µm absorption; no 2.0 µm band
- Notable deposits: Nili Fossae (exceptional concentration), Argyre, Hellas, Isidis rims
- Significance: Olivine weathers rapidly in water — its preservation indicates limited aqueous alteration since emplacement

### Plagioclase
- Widespread; dominant in northern lowlands
- Detected by TES and THEMIS thermal emission spectroscopy

## Phyllosilicates (Clay Minerals) — Noachian Signature

### Distribution
Exposed in Noachian-age crust globally. Concentrated at:
- Nili Fossae — highest diversity
- Mawrth Vallis — largest exposure
- Jezero Crater — delta deposits
- Eridania Basin — deep water alteration

### Types Detected (OMEGA/CRISM)
| Mineral | Spectral Feature | Setting |
|---------|-----------------|---------|
| Nontronite (Fe-smectite) | 2.29 µm, 1.42 µm | Most common; widespread |
| Saponite (Mg-smectite) | 2.31 µm | Altered basalt |
| Montmorillonite (Al-smectite) | 2.21 µm | Upper stratigraphy |
| Kaolinite | 2.16 µm, 2.21 µm | Intense weathering |
| Chlorite | 2.33 µm | Hydrothermal/metamorphic |
| Prehnite | 2.35 µm | Hydrothermal (>200°C) |
| Serpentine | 2.32 µm | Olivine alteration |
| Illite/muscovite | 2.20 µm | Diagenetic |

### Stratigraphic Pattern
Al-phyllosilicates (montmorillonite, kaolinite) typically overlie Fe/Mg-phyllosilicates (nontronite, saponite) — may record global weathering transition from neutral-alkaline to acidic conditions (Bishop et al. 2008).

### Formation Conditions
- Require liquid water at 0–300°C depending on type
- Fe/Mg smectites: moderate temperature, neutral pH
- Prehnite/chlorite: >200°C hydrothermal
- Kaolinite: acidic weathering or intense leaching

## Sulfates — Hesperian Signature

### Distribution
- Valles Marineris interior layered deposits
- Meridiani Planum (Opportunity)
- Gale Crater upper Murray Formation (Curiosity)
- North polar dune fields (gypsum)

### Types
| Mineral | Formula | Setting |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Kieserite | MgSO₄·H₂O | VM interior deposits |
| Gypsum | CaSO₄·2H₂O | North polar dunes |
| Jarosite | KFe₃(SO₄)₂(OH)₆ | Meridiani (acidic water) |
| Polyhydrated sulfates | MgSO₄·nH₂O | VM, widespread |

### Significance
- Record evaporation of sulfuric acid-rich brines
- Jarosite at Meridiani proves past acidic, oxidizing conditions
- Gale Crater phyllosilicate-to-sulfate transition records drying lake environment

## Iron Oxides

- **Hematite (Fe₂O₃)**: "Blueberries" at Meridiani (groundwater concretions); fine-grained gives Mars red color
- **Magnetite (Fe₃O₄)**: In some meteorites; possible biogenic origin debated
- **Goethite (FeOOH)**: Hydrated iron oxide; indicates past water
- **Ferrihydrite**: Amorphous; widespread in global dust

## Carbonates

- **Rare on surface** despite CO₂-rich atmosphere
- **Nili Fossae**: Magnesium carbonate (MgCO₃) — most significant exposure (Ehlmann et al. 2008)
- **ALH84001 meteorite**: Carbonate globules with controversial structures
- Scarcity likely from: acidic Hesperian conditions destroying carbonates, or limited formation

## Silica

- **Opaline silica**: Spirit found at Home Plate (Gusev) — hydrothermal or acid steam origin
- **Amorphous silica**: Widespread in Gale Crater
- **Quartz & kaolinite**: Perseverance found in Jezero (Feb 2026) — excellent biosignature preservers
- Significance: Silica is among the best biosignature preservation minerals on Earth

## Perchlorates

- ~0.5–1 wt% in soil (Phoenix, Curiosity)
- Lower water freezing point to −70°C (Mg-perchlorate)
- Toxic to life but some microbes can metabolize it
- Complicates organic detection: oxidizes organics during pyrolysis

## Organics on Mars

### Curiosity (Gale Crater)
- Thiophenes, benzene, toluene, chlorobenzene (2018)
- Decane, undecane, dodecane (2025) — largest organics found on Mars; possible fatty acid fragments
- Feb 2026: NASA confirmed non-biological sources cannot fully explain abundance

### Perseverance (Jezero)
- PAHs preserved in sulfate minerals (Sep 2025, Nature Astronomy)
- Organic carbon in Bright Angel formation mudstones
- "Cheyava Falls" sample: vivianite + greigite + organic carbon with "leopard spot" textures (Sep 2025, Nature) — potential biosignature

## CRISM Spectral Analysis Methods

### Key Band Parameters (Viviano-Beck et al. 2014)
| Parameter | Wavelength | Target |
|-----------|-----------|--------|
| OLINDEX3 | 1.08–1.81 µm | Olivine |
| LCPINDEX2 | 1.08–2.38 µm | Low-Ca pyroxene |
| HCPINDEX2 | 1.08–2.38 µm | High-Ca pyroxene |
| BD1500_2 | 1.50 µm | H₂O ice |
| BD2100_2 | 2.10 µm | H₂O / monohydrated |
| BD2200 | 2.20 µm | Al-OH (kaolinite) |
| BD2290 | 2.29 µm | Fe/Mg-OH (nontronite) |
| BD2300 | 2.30 µm | Mg-OH (saponite) |
| MIN2295_2480 | 2.295/2.48 µm | Kaolinite + Al-smectite |

### Ice Detection Signatures
- 1.5 µm H₂O absorption (band depth >0.02 for confident detection)
- 2.0 µm H₂O absorption
- 1.25 µm Fresnel reflection peak
- Dusty ice reduces band depth below 0.01 (Langevin et al. 2007)
