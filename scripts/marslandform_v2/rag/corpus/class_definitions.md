# Mars Landform Class Definitions and Core Literature

## LDA (Lobate Debris Apron)
### Definition
LDA are lobate, viscous-flow-like accumulations commonly draped along the bases of steep scarps, mesas, and massifs in fretted terrain. Their planform is typically apron-shaped and radially to downslope spreading from source escarpments.

### Diagnostic Indicators
- Apron geometry attached to scarp or massif base.
- Convex to gently sloping profiles with lobate margins.
- Surface lineations and debris-mantled textures consistent with ice-rich creep.

### Key References
- Squyres (1979): early interpretation of viscous flow and ice-rich debris morphology.
- Chuang and Crown (2005): detailed geomorphic mapping of debris aprons and related features.
- Li et al. (2005): morphologic analysis supporting ice-related deformation.
- Head et al. (2010): integrated interpretation of regional glacial/periglacial assemblages.

## LVF (Lineated Valley Fill)
### Definition
LVF is valley-confined, lineated material interpreted as debris-covered, ice-rich flow occupying troughs and fretted valleys. Flow fabrics commonly track valley axes and may integrate tributaries.

### Diagnostic Indicators
- Strong confinement by valley walls.
- Longitudinal lineations, flow bands, and tributary junction patterns.
- Low-gradient valley-floor occupancy with coherent down-valley organization.

### Key References
- Head et al. (2006): evidence for extensive valley glaciation and flow structures.
- Head et al. (2010): broader climatic and geomorphic context linking LVF to mid-latitude glaciation.

## CCF (Concentric Crater Fill)
### Definition
CCF consists of crater-interior, concentric to arcuate ridge-and-trough patterns interpreted as deformation of ice-rich fill under topographic confinement.

### Diagnostic Indicators
- Occurs primarily within crater basins.
- Concentric ridges/troughs centered on crater geometry.
- Surface textures and topographic organization distinct from valley-parallel LVF flow.

### Key References
- Levy et al. (2009): regional mapping and interpretation of concentric crater fill populations.
- Levy et al. (2010): morphometric and process-oriented analysis of crater-confined icy deposits.

## GLF (Glacier-Like Form)
### Definition
GLF are relatively small, alpine-style glacier analogs with alcove/headwall source regions and short tongue-like bodies, often exhibiting moraine-like arcuate ridges.

### Diagnostic Indicators
- Compact size, frequently less than about 10 km scale.
- Steep headwalls near approximately 30 degrees transitioning to gentler tongues.
- Distinct terminus morphologies and nested arcuate ridge sets.

### Key References
- Hubbard et al. (2011): glacier flow and structural modeling in Martian contexts.
- Souness et al. (2012): synthesis of glacier-like forms and climatic implications.
- Brough et al. (2019): updated inventories and morphological constraints on GLF evolution.

## Cross-Class Notes for Automated Retrieval
### Shared traits
- All classes can include ice-related textures and low-relief debris mantles.
- Transitional morphologies are common near topographic boundaries.

### Disambiguation hierarchy
- First use geomorphic container (crater, valley, scarp apron, alcove).
- Then use planform organization (concentric, valley-parallel, radial apron, compact tongue).
- Finally refine with slope, roughness, and texture diagnostics.
